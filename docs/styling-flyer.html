Screamer · the styling layer · powered by Fireworks

Dictation that writes like you, not like AI.

Speech-to-text is messy. The obvious fix — ask an LLM to "clean it up" — makes it sound like a robot. We built a harness that fixes the mess and strips the AI tells, in your own voice, while keeping exactly what you meant.

The problem

Raw dictation is full of filler, false starts, and run-ons ("um so i think we should uh ship it"). Hand it to a frontier model with a naive "clean this up" prompt and you get fluent text — but littered with slop: the stylistic fingerprints of AI writing. Worse, big models quietly change what you meant — hardening a "maybe" into a certainty, dropping a "sorry," inventing detail.

What is "slop"?

The tell-tale markers of machine writing — grounded in Kobak et al. 2025 (measuring excess "AI vocabulary"). We detect and remove:

em / en dashes — buzzwords: delve, leverage, seamless, robust "not X, but Y" rule-of-three lists signposts: "at the end of the day" rhetorical questions ", which …" clause-stitching

What we do

An anti-slop harness on a small open model (on Fireworks) rewrites the raw transcript into clean written text. It's not a fine-tune — it's a harness, which is cheap, instant, and portable:

Anti-slop prompt

Strict rules: fix filler & punctuation, ban every AI tell, keep the speaker's voice and register.

Domain glossary

Fixes clear mis-transcriptions (e.g. "stylus tick layer" → "stylistic layer") without forcing terms.

Slop gate

Scores the output and preserves intent; returns text unchanged when it's already clean.

How we evaluate it

Three metrics on a held-out set of 120 dictations, all on Fireworks (models + a neutral judge, so we never grade our own homework) via Fireworks' Eval Protocol:

Slop / 1k

Rules-based detector (Kobak-grounded), kept in exact parity between the app (Rust) and the eval (Python). Lower is better.

Intent fidelity

LLM judge: did the rewrite keep every claim, hedge, and speech act? 0–1, higher better.

Writing quality

LLM judge: does it read like something you'd have typed, in your voice? 0–1, higher better.

How we improve it — the proof

Head-to-head vs a frontier-class open model (Kimi K3), same inputs, with bootstrap 95% CIs and McNemar's exact test:

Approachslop/1kintentqualitylatency
Frontier model, naive prompt27.50.920.875.6 s
Frontier model + our harness3.50.980.958.5 s
Ours — small model + harness8.40.980.932.7 s
  • We beat the frontier model's out-of-box output on every axis — slop, intent, and quality — all statistically significant (slop-free rate 74% vs 47%, McNemar p = 1.4×10⁻⁵), at ~2× lower latency.
  • Against the frontier model running our same harness, we match it on intent and quality at ~3× the speed (2.7 s vs 8.5 s) — the frontier model is a touch cleaner, the honest tradeoff we disclose.
  • The harness is the lever: it cuts slop ~8× on any model.

See it

Raw dictation (what you said)
hey um i talked to jenny this morning and she said the uh the delivery is gonna be late again like maybe thursday instead of tuesday i mean wednesday sorry and she asked if you can call her back before noon
Frontier model, naive prompt slop + intent drift
I talked to Jenny this morning. She said the delivery is going to be late again — it's now expected Thursday instead of Wednesday. She asked if you could call her back before noon.
Ours — small model + harness clean + faithful
So I talked to Jenny this morning, and she said the delivery is going to be late again, maybe Thursday instead of Wednesday, sorry. She asked if you can call her back before noon.

The frontier model adds an em-dash and quietly hardens "maybe Thursday" into a certainty, dropping the "sorry." Ours keeps the hedge, the apology, and your voice — with no AI tells.

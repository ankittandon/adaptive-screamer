Adaptive Screamer · Fireworks: Deploy
Deploy submission · open models that adapt to you

One dictation app, adapted to how you speak and how you write.

Screamer is a free, private, on-device Mac dictation app. We make it adaptable at two layers — the ear (ASR tuned to your voice, including dysarthric speech) and the pen (a styling harness that writes like you, not like AI). At both layers a specialized open model or harness beats a frontier baseline, proven with McNemar's test and the bootstrap. Here is the playbook in six steps.

Headline. The ear: dysarthric WER 42.7% → 22.3% on held-out speech (McNemar p=1.4×10⁻¹³), running on-device. The pen: our small open model + harness beats a frontier model's out-of-box styling on slop, intent, and quality — all significant — at ~2× lower latency. Both on open models; the pen on Fireworks, the ear on the NVIDIA / on-device stack.

Disclosure (rules §4). Screamer itself is pre-existing work (a fork of suvamsh/screamer, MIT). Built for this event: the dysarthric ASR fine-tune + on-device deployment, the styling head-to-head, and the full evaluation (leakage-free splits, bootstrap + McNemar). Developed with the Claude Code agent; the team is responsible for all reported results.

Step 1
Find a task worth doing

Dictation should adapt to the individual. Two layers, two underserved jobs:

The ear — ASR

Transcribe speech accurately, including dysarthric speech (cerebral palsy, ALS, stroke) that general ASR fails on. ~3M+ US adults have a speech disability.

The pen — styling

Turn raw transcripts into clean written text in your own voice, without the AI "slop" a naive LLM adds. Every dictation, every app.

Why open models. Frontier ASR gets ~40–55% WER on dysarthric speech; a fine-tuned open model is more accurate, free at the margin, private, and the weights are yours. For styling, a small open model + a harness matches a frontier model at a fraction of the cost. Business value: replaces a paid cloud dictation subscription (≈$12–15/mo) at $0, keeps sensitive speech on-device, and — the part that matters most — makes dictation usable for people it currently excludes.

Step 2
Baseline on open models

Lock the test sets first, keep every baseline fair (same prompt/settings), and make everything beat these numbers.

  • Ear: base whisper-base.en scores 42.7% WER on 181 held-out dysarthric utterances; NVIDIA's open Parakeet-TDT (via NIM) scores 60.3% — frontier-class open ASR fails here too.
  • Pen: frontier-class open model kimi-k3 with a naive "clean this up" prompt, on 120 held-out synthetic dictations.

Splits are leakage-free: ASR holds out whole utterances, unseen vocabulary, and an unseen speaker; styling uses a fixed held-out set scored by a neutral judge (qwen-max — neither contestant), on Fireworks' Eval Protocol.

Step 4
Build it

Ear

SFT of whisper-base.en on TORGO (HuggingFace, NVIDIA GPU), converted to GGML, shipped on-device in Screamer's whisper.cpp/Metal stack as a selectable model.

Pen

An anti-slop harness on a small open model on Fireworks — a strict system prompt (grounded in Kobak et al. 2025), a domain glossary, and a slop gate. No fine-tune needed.

Step 6
Prove the gains are real

Two tests, as the rules ask — they answer different questions, so we run both. McNemar asks whether a change wins more cases than it loses (needs a pass/fail per case). The bootstrap asks how big the gain is and whether it would survive a different test set (works on any score).

*On unseen words the WER drop is significant (bootstrap), while the stricter whole-utterance exact-match rate rises +5.7 pts at p=0.08 — McNemar is all-or-nothing, WER gives partial credit.

Honest caveat on clustering. Our ASR utterances cluster by speaker, and we have only three. So the per-utterance tests are slightly overconfident (they treat utterances as independent), and a speaker-level bootstrap isn't reliable with three units. We therefore lean on the pattern holding across all three independent splits rather than on one over-precise p-value. The styling dictations are independent, so no clustering issue there.
